# Layered & Overlapping Shapes to Eliminate Anti-Aliasing Render Seams

Started 2026-09-02 00h45. Session planned.

## Problem Statement

When SVG vector shapes are generated as adjacent non-overlapping polygons (with inner holes cut out using `fill-rule="evenodd"`), web browser SVG renderers and canvas rasterizers exhibit subpixel anti-aliasing artifacts along shared borders. Because the boundary edge between two adjacent shapes splits pixel opacity between the two paths, subpixel rounding leads to tiny hairline gaps, seams, or background color bleeding ("huecos al renderizar").

## Proposed Solution: Layered / Overlapping Stacking Hierarchy

Instead of emitting strictly adjacent shapes where outer surrounding regions cut out inner holes:
1. **Topological Depth & Containment**: Detect region enclosure hierarchy (which region surrounds/encloses which).
2. **Solid Outer Layers**: Outer/surrounding shapes do not subtract/cut out inner enclosed shapes (or only subtract transparent background holes). They are rendered as solid shapes underneath their enclosed children.
3. **Z-Order Stacking**: Surrounding shapes are emitted first (bottom layer), and enclosed shapes are emitted after (top layer).
4. **Seam Elimination**: The anti-aliased edge of the top shape renders directly over the solid color of the underlying shape, completely eliminating rendering gaps and seams.

# Extension 2026-09-02 01h20 — Elimination of White Seam Lines via Union Layer Boundary Extraction

## Root Cause Analysis of White Border Lines around Pixels

1. **Why White Lines Occur**:
   - The user provided a screenshot showing 1px white/light halo lines along pixel square edges.
   - Previously, underlying background regions ($R_0$) carved out notches for adjacent pixel shapes ($R_1$) along their **outer** boundary ring.
   - Because $R_0$ carved out a notch for $R_1$, the canvas behind $R_1$ was empty (white).
   - Subpixel rendering / anti-aliasing along $R_1$'s border blended $R_1$'s color with the empty white canvas behind it, creating visible white seam lines around the pixels.

2. **Topological Solution (`src/svg.rs`)**:
   - For each layer rank $target\_pos$ in Z-order ($0 \dots N-1$), extract the outer boundary of the **union of all regions at or above rank $target\_pos$**: $U = \{ R_j \mid pos\_in\_z[j] \ge target\_pos \}$.
   - A half-edge $e$ is an external boundary edge of layer $R_i$ if and only if one side of $e$ is inside $U$ ($pos\_in\_z \ge target\_pos$) and the other side is outside $U$ ($pos\_in\_z < target\_pos$ or canvas boundary).
   - Internal edges between $R_i$ and top regions $R_j$ ($pos\_in\_z[j] > target\_pos$) are completely IGNORED when constructing $R_i$'s base layer contour.
   - **Result**: Underlying background layers ($R_i$) render as a 100% solid paper cutout underneath all top shapes ($R_j$), providing solid background fill directly behind $R_j$'s pixel edges and completely eliminating white seam lines.
