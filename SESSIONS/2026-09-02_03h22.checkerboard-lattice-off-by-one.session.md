# Checkerboard removal leaves the background behind

Date: 2026-09-02 03h22

## Report

A pixel-art PNG with a baked-in transparency checkerboard (Sonic sprite,
1632x2564) comes out of `pixelart` with the checkerboard still there — not
untouched, but partially erased, which is worse: the leftovers keep the period
of the checkerboard and then confuse `grid::detect`.

## Reproduction

The reported image is not in the repo, so the case was rebuilt from
`examples/results-to-improve/Sonic1.png` (1800x2823, real alpha) composited over
a white/`#cccccc` checkerboard, optionally rescaled the way a downloaded copy
would be. Harness in `.tmp/repro` (throwaway crate that depends on the lib by
path); it reports, per case, the detected cell and how much of the true
background is left opaque.

Before any change:

| case | cell | coverage | background left |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| cell 8, no rescale | 8.00 | 12.3% | **62.8%** |
| cell 12, no rescale | 12.00 | 26.5% | 19.9% |
| cell 16, no rescale | 16.00 | 26.7% | 19.3% |
| cell 20, no rescale | 20.00 | 30.8% | 6.8% |
| cell 24, no rescale | 24.00 | 30.9% | 6.6% |

A checkerboard with *nothing drawn on it* is erased 100%, so the geometry fit is
not the suspect — the interaction with the drawing is.

## Diagnosis

`Lattice::cell_at` floors `(x - offset) / cell` on the pixel *index*.

`axis_fit` derives `offset` as a circular mean over hundreds of thousands of run
starts, so it never lands on the exact integer: in the repro it came out
`0.0055`. That is enough to move every cell boundary a hair past the integer, so
cell *k* covers pixels `[8k+1 .. 8k+8]` instead of `[8k .. 8k+7]` — the whole
lattice is displaced one pixel.

Displaced, each cell straddles four true cells: 49 pixels of its own tone, 14 of
the opposite one, 1 of the diagonal. Agreement drops to 50/64 = **0.78**, under
the `CELL_AGREEMENT = 0.9` the cell has to clear, so almost no cell is confirmed
and `erase` has nowhere to seed. Instrumented run on cell 8:

```
fit: cell (7.99996, 7.99986) offset (0.0055, 0.0290) flip true
cells 226x354: agrees 10672/80004 ok 9671
casilla que contiene (4,4): 50/64
```

Because the fitted cell is a hair under 8.0, the displacement even cancels out
past x≈1104 and y≈1624, which is why the image comes out erased in patches
rather than uniformly — the "partially removed" symptom.

The pure-checkerboard test passes because with no drawing every run start is an
exact multiple of the cell, the circular mean lands on 0.0 exactly, and the
off-by-one never triggers. That is why no test caught this.

## Change

`Lattice::cell_at` measures the **centre** of the pixel:

```rust
((x as f64 + 0.5 - self.offset.0) / self.cell.0).floor() as i64
```

`offset` marks a boundary at the left edge of a pixel, so the centre sits half a
pixel inside its own cell and the mapping now tolerates ±0.5 px of error in the
fitted phase instead of breaking on ±0.001.

After the change, every non-rescaled case erases the background completely:

| case | cell | coverage | background left |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| cell 8..24, no rescale | exact | 33.1% | **0.0%** |
| cell 12 -> 1632x2564 | 10.88 | 28.5% | 13.4% |
| cell 16 -> 1632x2564 | 14.50 | 29.0% | 11.9% |
| cell 24 -> 1632x2564 | 21.76 | 30.7% | 6.8% |

The corpus snapshot improves too: `corpus-damero` goes from 385 paths /
1057 subpaths / 16% checkerboard to 375 / 1033 / 17%.

## Still open: rescaled checkerboards

When the image has been resampled, the boundary between two cells is a blend of
the two tones. `label` calls those pixels "neither" (2), which
1. lowers per-cell agreement below 0.9, and
2. blocks the `erase` flood, so a one-pixel mesh with the checkerboard period
   survives even where detection works.

A tried-out prototype (kept in `.tmp/repro/checker.fix2-mezcla.rs`) adds a
fourth label for "blend of the two tones": neutral for the cell agreement,
passable but not seedable for the flood, skipped when `runs` checks alternation.
It cuts the leftovers on the 1632x2564 cases to 0.1–3.3%, but it makes `runs`
stricter and some heavy downscales (0.5x) stop being detected at all. Not
applied — it needs the real image to be tuned against rather than a synthetic
guess.

## Walkthrough

- `src/checker.rs` — `Lattice::cell_at` now maps a pixel to its cell by the
  pixel centre (`x + 0.5`) instead of by its index, so a sub-pixel error in the
  fitted phase no longer displaces the whole lattice by one pixel. The doc
  comment on the method records why the half pixel is there, so nobody
  "simplifies" it back.
- `tests/golden/corpus-damero.svg` — regenerated. It is the only snapshot that
  moves: 385 -> 375 paths, 1057 -> 1033 subpaths, checkerboard coverage
  16% -> 17%, 31403 -> 30683 bytes. Fewer paths because the leftover mesh of
  checkerboard pixels that used to survive is gone.
- Full suite green, `cargo fmt --check` clean. The one clippy warning
  (`Default for Config`, `src/lib.rs:88`) predates this change.

Not done, deliberately: the rescaled-checkerboard weakness described above. It
wants the real reported image to tune against.

Suggested commit message:

## Extension 2026-09-02 21h40 — the real image, and why one global parity cannot work

The reported image turned out to be the corpus one,
`Gemini_Generated_Image_crfa7acrfa7acrfa.png` (1632x2564), so it already has a
golden snapshot. Measured on the bench with the off-by-one fix in:

- the real background is 31.4% of the image (flood from the border through the
  band spanned by the two tones),
- **46.1% of it survives** the removal.

### What the leftovers are

Not a colour problem: the surviving pixels are the two tones exactly —
`#ffffff` (135678 px), `#d9d9d9`/`#dadada`/`#dbdbdb` (131k) — all labelled
correctly. Classifying every surviving pixel:

```
paridad al revés 653804   casilla sin confirmar 298653   ajenos al damero 2493094
```

So the dominant cause is **inverted parity**: the cell is recognised, its tone is
recognised, and `expected()` asks for the other one.

The diagnostic map (`.tmp/mapa-damero.png`, green = erased, red = parity
inverted) shows a clean **diagonal** boundary: the whole upper half and the right
side are inverted, the lower left is erased. Diagonal because parity is
`(cx + cy) & 1`, so an error in either axis moves the same line.

### Why the period cannot be pinned down

Measuring the checkerboard directly, with sub-pixel crossings on clean lines:

| axis | true step | fitted | concentration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| x (rows) | ~40.99 | 40.884 | 0.999 |
| y (columns) | ~40.53–40.61 | 40.312 | 0.948 |

The y concentration is visibly worse, and per band it peaks at a **different**
period each time: 40.80 in y ∈ [0, 700), 40.15 in [900, 1600), 40.63 in
[1800, 2500) — each with 0.98–1.00 concentration locally. The vertical period of
this image is not constant.

Forcing other periods makes it worse, not better, so the fit is not making a
mistake — 40.31 really is the best single answer:

| forced cell_y | background left |
| --- | --- |
| 40.31 (fitted) | 43.6% |
| 40.61 | 80.8% |
| 40.75 | 81.1% |
| 40.50, 40.89 | detection fails outright |

The arithmetic says a global parity is unreachable. For `(cx + cy) & 1` to stay
right across the image, the accumulated drift has to stay under half a cell:
that needs the y period accurate to ±0.16 px, and the image is not even
self-consistent to ±0.3.

## Planned change: read each cell's tone instead of computing it

The lattice keeps telling us **where** the cells are, which it does well —
alignment is fine, the map shows clean blocks and not mush. What it cannot do is
say **which tone** each one should be. So stop asking it:

1. `qualifying_cells` counts both tones per cell and takes the dominant one as
   that cell's tone, when it dominates by `CELL_AGREEMENT` over enough solid
   (non-`MIX`) pixels.
2. A cell is confirmed when at least two of its four neighbours carry the
   *other* tone. That is the same alternation test as today, but read off the
   image rather than derived from `(cx + cy) & 1`.
3. Cells that no tone dominates get one inferred by breadth-first search from
   the confirmed ones, alternating at each step, so the flood in `erase` still
   has a tone to compare against everywhere. Parity is then anchored **locally**
   and re-anchors on both sides of a drift, instead of being decided once for
   the whole image.
4. `Lattice::flip`, `expected()` and the `score()` pass in `fit` that chose the
   global parity all disappear.

Carried over from the earlier prototype, now with the real image to justify it:

- a fourth label, `MIX`, for colours that lie between the two tones — the border
  a resample leaves. Neutral for the cell agreement (excluded from the count),
  passable but not seedable for the flood in `erase`, so the one-pixel mesh with
  the checkerboard period stops surviving.
- `CELL_SOLID`, so a cell that is nearly all `MIX` cannot be confirmed on the
  four clean pixels it has left.
- `runs` grouped per line, and the cell measured **start to start** rather than
  by run length: a resample blurs both ends of every run and eats a pixel off
  each, and that bias does not cancel.

## Walkthrough (extension of 2026-09-02 21h40)

`src/checker.rs`, all of it in service of "the lattice says where, the image says
which tone":

- `Lattice` loses `flip`, and `expected()` is gone. `fit` no longer runs the
  `score(true) vs score(false)` pass that used to pick one parity for the whole
  image.
- `read_board` replaces `qualifying_cells`. It counts both tones per cell, takes
  the dominant one when it clears `CELL_AGREEMENT` over at least `CELL_SOLID` of
  the cell, and confirms the cell when two of its four neighbours carry the
  other tone. It returns a `Board` (tones, confirmed cells, `matching`).
- `spread_tones` fills in the cells no tone dominated, breadth-first from the
  confirmed ones, alternating at each step. That is what gives `erase` something
  to compare against everywhere while keeping the anchor local.
- `MIX`, `OTHER` and `blended()`: a fourth label for colours on the segment
  between the two tones — the border a resample leaves. Excluded from the cell
  count, passable but not seedable in `erase`.
- `runs` groups by line and looks **past** `MIX` when checking that a strip
  alternates. Without this, a resampled image has no qualifying runs at all:
  the strip next door is not the other tone, it is the gradient leading to it.
  This was the difference between four synthetic cases detecting and not.
- `axis_fit` measures the cell **start to start** instead of by run length. A
  resample blurs both ends of every strip and eats a pixel off each; the bias
  does not cancel.
- `trim_seam`, a pass after the flood: erases the one-pixel line left on the
  seam between two cells, where the sub-pixel step makes the first row of the
  new cell keep the old cell's tone. Deliberately without propagation — the
  version that propagated ate the white plane in
  `respeta_un_plano_blanco_del_dibujo`.

## Extension 2026-09-02 23h55 — "it removes the background with the option unchecked"

Reported: the background comes out removed even with the option unchecked.
Traced the whole chain before changing anything, and **every link honours both
flags**:

- library: `cargo test` covers it (`damero_conservado`), and by hand on the
  reported image — `--keep-checkerboard` keeps it (340 paths / 1476 subpaths vs
  336 / 699).
- wasm: probed `convertRgba` / `convertIllustration` directly from node.
  `removeCheckerboard: false` keeps the checkerboard; `removeBackground` is
  `false` by default, and only with `true` does the flat background go and the
  canvas crop.
- page: `web/views/modes.jsx` maps `removeCheckerboard: v.removeChecker` and
  `removeBackground: v.removeBackground`, defaults `true` / `false`; `Check` in
  `web/components/inputs.jsx` passes `e.currentTarget.checked`, a real boolean.

So there is no wiring bug to fix. What there is: **"Damero" is a separate
checkbox and it is on by default**, and on an image whose background *is* the
checkerboard, that option is what empties it — now much more thoroughly than
before this session. Unchecking "Damero" keeps it. Which of the two behaviours
is wanted is a decision, not a defect, and it is asked rather than guessed.

Two real gaps found on the way, and closed:

- `scripts/web-smoke.mjs` did not exercise `removeCheckerboard` or
  `removeBackground` at all — exactly the kind of key the file exists to pin,
  since a typo in one does not fail, it silently falls back to the default.
  Added: the checkerboard case for the three states of `removeCheckerboard`, and
  a flat-background case for the three states of `removeBackground`, in both
  segmentations. Nine checks.
- `tests/checker.rs` gains `no_confunde_unas_bandas_con_un_damero`. Stripes
  alternate two tones like a checkerboard but only along one axis. They used to
  be ruled out by the parity — `(cx + cy) & 1` changes on both axes and a stripe
  does not — and reading the tone per cell removed that guarantee: a stripe cell
  really does have two neighbours of the other tone. They are still rejected,
  by `fit`, but that is now the only thing standing between a banded background
  and being erased, so it is pinned.

`make test` green.

## Extension 2026-09-03 00h20 — one checkbox instead of two

empties the checkerboard if there is one and the flat colour if there is not.

Scope: **the page only**. The library keeps two independent flags, because they

where "two boxes for what looks like one thing" is a problem.

- `web/views/modes.jsx`: pixelart defaults lose `removeChecker`; `options(v)`

  finds nothing left to do.

  fondo" is rewritten to cover both.

With the box unchecked, an image with a checkerboard now converts **with the
checkerboard in it**. That is the point of the change, and it is what the page
did not do before. It also means the page and `vektro pixelart` no longer agree
by default: the CLI still removes the checkerboard unless told
`--keep-checkerboard`. Left that way rather than changed quietly — flipping a
CLI default is a separate decision.

## Extension 2026-09-03 01h10 — the cut is still not clean

Reported with a screenshot: a white fringe survives around the figure and in a
band along the bottom. Located it — the leftovers are not around the silhouette,
they are **bands at the edges of the image**:

```
58720 px  caja 0,2382 .. 1631,2563   (1632x182, the whole bottom)
19719 px  caja 1490,648 .. 1631,972  (right edge)
13508 px  caja 0,447 .. 58,894       (left edge)
 3119 px  caja 1102,0 .. 1223,39     (top edge)
```

182 rows is four and a half cells. That is the drift again, in its remaining
form: the fitted step is 40.31 against a true ~40.6, so by the bottom of a
2564 px image the lattice is **half a cell out of phase**. Each lattice cell
there is half of one true cell and half of the next, no tone dominates, nothing
is confirmed and the flood has nothing to match. Reading the tone per cell
turned a wrong answer into no answer; it did not make the geometry right.

### Change: read the cell boundaries too

The step comes out of `axis_fit` with two decimals, and over sixty cells that
rounding walks off. So stop counting cells and **look at them**: on the row where
one cell ends, nearly every background pixel changes tone at once, which is a
peak that stands out from far away.

`boundaries()` per axis: count, for each line, how many pixels change tone
against the previous line; then walk from the first boundary predicting
`previous + cell` and snapping each prediction to the best peak within about a
third of a cell. Snapping **from the snapped previous one** is the point — each
step re-anchors, so the window only ever has to cover one step's error (0.3 px)
instead of the accumulated one, and a period that drifts is followed instead of
averaged. Where the drawing hides a boundary there is no peak and the prediction
stands, which is the right fallback.

`Lattice` then carries a coordinate → cell-index array per axis and `cell_at` is
two lookups, so it also stops doing float division per pixel.

### Walkthrough (01h10)

`src/checker.rs`:

- `boundaries()` — new. Per axis, counts for each line how many background pixels
  change tone against the previous line, then chains predictions snapping each to
  the strongest peak nearby.
- `Lattice` stops being `(cell, offset)` arithmetic and carries `column` / `row`,
  one cell index per coordinate. `cell_at` is two lookups; `size()` replaces the
  origin/last arithmetic in `cell_index`, which no longer needs `w`/`h`. `cell`
  stays for the report only.

One thing had to be added after measuring. Snapping to any peak at all made the
synthetic bench **worse**, from 0.0% leftovers to as much as 24.5%: with a small
cell the window is a pixel or two, the peaks of a resampled image are weak, and
one bad snap drags every boundary after it. So a peak now has to earn it — the
median boundary marks about as many lines as there is background, and half of
that is the bar. Below it the prediction stands, which is exactly right for a
checkerboard of constant step, where the arithmetic was already correct.
