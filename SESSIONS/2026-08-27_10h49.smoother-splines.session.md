# Smoother Splines for Circular Arcs

Started 2026-08-27 10h49. Planned; awaiting user approval.

## Context & Objectives

The user reports that an image made of straight lines and **perfect circular arcs**
converts to a good but not perfect SVG: the result would be perfect if the splines
`Fit::Spline` emits were smoother.

> Note: no image was attached to the request. The diagnosis below is based on the
> code in [src/fit/spline.rs](src/fit/spline.rs) and on the synthetic circle case
> already covered by `un_circulo_sale_liso` in [tests/fit.rs](tests/fit.rs#L552).
> If the actual image is provided, the plan should be re-validated against it
> before or after implementation.

Goal: make circular arcs come out as continuous cubic runs instead of a mix of a
few cubics and many chords, without breaking the tolerance promise (no contour
point further than `tolerance` from what is drawn) and without rounding real
corners.

---

## Diagnosis

The documented behaviour is already the symptom. From the `SPLINE_TOLERANCE`
table in [src/fit.rs](src/fit.rs#L145), a radius-30 circle currently emits:

| tolerance | segments |
| --- | --- |
| 0.75 | 2 curves + 108 lines |
| 1.0 | 6 + 36 |
| **1.5** | **4 curves + 4 lines** |

Even at the sweet spot, **half of the circle is drawn with straight chords**.
That is exactly what "not smooth enough" looks like when zoomed in: flat facets
with a tangent break at each end.

Four contributing causes, in order of impact:

### 1. `straight()` short-circuits before any curve is attempted

In `fit()` the very first test on every piece is:

```rust
if b - a < 2 || straight(&pts[a..=b], tolerance) { /* emit a line */ }
```

`straight()` only measures **sagitta against the chord**. Any arc piece whose
sagitta happens to be under 1.5 px is emitted as a line, regardless of how much
it actually turns. On a raster contour that condition is met constantly for
short pieces, so arcs get chopped into chords. This is the main cause.

### 2. Tangents are estimated from two points on a staircase

`forward` / `backward` / `center` / `across` all compute `unit(p[i±WINDOW] - p[i])`,
a single difference between two lattice points. On a digitised contour that
direction is quantised and noisy. Since `bezier()` fixes the tangent directions
and only solves for the two control-handle lengths, a wrong tangent is a wrong
curve that no amount of least-squares can fix — it just raises the error and
forces a split, which produces more joints and more visible breaks.

### 3. `merge_lines()` + RDP re-polygonises

Runs of consecutive line segments are re-simplified with RDP. Correct in itself,
but it cements the spurious lines produced by (1) into the output instead of
letting them become curves.

### 4. `REPARAM = 2` is low

Reparameterisation is cheap compared to a split. Two passes is where it "stops
improving" for the current tangent quality; with better tangents (2) a couple
more passes convert would-be splits into a single well-fitted cubic.

---

## Plan

### Step 1 — Least-squares tangents instead of two-point differences

Replace the two-point tangent estimators with a direction fitted over the whole
window:

- Add `tangent_ls(pts, at, from, to)` computing a weighted sum of the
  differences `pts[j] - pts[at]` across the window (linearly decaying weights),
  which is a first-order least-squares direction and averages out the lattice
  staircase.
- Keep `WINDOW = 4` as the span. Orientation is taken from the traversal
  direction, so `forward`, `backward`, `center` and `across` become thin wrappers.
- Cadenas cortas: the window still clamps to the piece bounds exactly as today,
  so no behaviour change for 3-point chains.

Expected effect: tangent noise drops, `deviation()` drops, fewer splits.

### Step 2 — Turn-aware straightness test

Replace the unconditional `straight(piece, tolerance)` short-circuit with a test
that a piece is a line only when it is straight **and** it does not turn:

```text
is_line(piece, t0, t1) =
      straight(piece, tolerance)
  &&  angle_between(t0, -t1) <= FLAT_TURN
```

with a new constant `FLAT_TURN` (proposed: cosine of ~12°, i.e. `0.978`). A real
raster straight edge has entry and exit tangents essentially anti-parallel, so it
still becomes a line — including 45° staircases, which is what keeps rectangles
and diagonals corner-clean. A shallow arc piece turns measurably and now gets a
cubic even though its sagitta is small.

This is the change that removes the "4 curves + 4 lines" split.

`merge_lines()` is left untouched: with fewer spurious lines it simply has less
to do.

### Step 3 — Raise `REPARAM`

Bump `REPARAM` from 2 to 4, with the early-exit on `err <= tolerance²` already in
place so well-behaved pieces cost nothing extra. Re-measure and keep the lowest
value that no longer changes the segment counts.

### Step 4 — Guard the handle lengths

`bezier()` currently falls back to `chord/3` for both handles when the solve is
degenerate or returns a non-positive alpha. Add an upper clamp as well: an alpha
larger than the chord length produces overshoot and a visible bulge. Clamp to
`dist(p0, p3)` and re-check. This is a correctness guard, not a tuning knob.

### Step 5 — Re-measure `SPLINE_TOLERANCE`

The 1.5 default was chosen *because* the fitter was chasing quantisation noise
below 1.0. With Steps 1–2 that reason may no longer hold, and a lower default
would track the source geometry more closely. Re-run the radius-30 circle table
and update the constant and its doc comment with the new measurements. Do not
change the number without the table.

### Step 6 — Tests

Extend [tests/fit.rs](tests/fit.rs):

- `un_circulo_sale_solo_de_curvas`: radius-30 circle must emit **zero** line
  segments; every joint tangent-continuous (reuse the existing cross-product
  check); and every sampled point of the emitted path within `SPLINE_TOLERANCE`
  of the true circle. This last assertion is the one that actually pins
  "smooth", since segment counts alone can be gamed.
- Keep `un_rectangulo_no_lleva_curvas` and the shared-boundary tests green:
  they are the guard against Step 2 rounding real corners.
- Run `cargo test` plus the golden/corpus tests; golden output **will** change,
  so regenerate and inspect the diff rather than accepting it blindly.

---

## Risks

- **Rounding real corners.** Step 2 emits curves where lines were emitted before.
  Mitigated by `inner_corners` still cutting at >60° turns, and by the rectangle
  test.
- **File size.** A cubic costs six numbers, a line two. Replacing lines with
  curves grows the SVG. The corpus size numbers must be reported, and if the
  growth is large the trade-off should be a decision, not a surprise.
- **Golden churn.** All spline golden output changes. Expected and intended.

# Extension 2026-08-27 11h05 — Image received, root cause measured

Summary of what this extension adds: the reference image was provided, the
current output was measured against it, and the measurement **overturns the
priority of the original plan**. The dominant defect is not the chord
short-circuit but the **unbounded angular span of a single cubic**. The plan is
revised accordingly.

## The image

`.tmp/proxyxt--proxy-web-client-for-browser--logo-withou.png`, 1024x1024: a flat
logo on a black field. Orange hook, white "r" with a circular bump, a white dot,
a blue rounded square, a black circular notch. Its geometry is exactly as
described: **every edge is either axis-aligned straight or a circular arc.**
There is not a single oblique straight edge in the design. That makes it a clean
oracle — any oblique line in the output is an artefact, and any deviation from a
circle is fitter error.

## Measurement 1 — segment census

`cargo run --release -- illustration <img> --fit spline`, counting commands
inside `d="..."` only:

| config | cubics | oblique lines | h/v | bytes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| default (simplify 5), tol 1.0 | 46 | 21 | 19 | 2250 |
| default, tol 1.5 | 42 | 21 | 20 | 2101 |
| default, tol 2.0 | 36 | 19 | 20 | 1881 |
| `--no-simplify`, tol 1.0 | 54 | 29 | 24 | 2663 |
| `--no-simplify`, tol 1.5 | 46 | 23 | 25 | 2324 |
| `--no-simplify`, tol 2.0 | 46 | 21 | 23 | 2298 |

Breaking the 21 oblique lines of the default run down by length (they come in
pairs, because a shared boundary is written by both of its faces):

| length | count | what they are |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 235.0, 156.6, 93.1, 48.7, 39.7, 29.9 | 12 | near-axis-aligned real straight edges, oblique only because of the subpixel offset. **Legitimate.** |
| 11.7, 9.6 | 4 | chords in the middle of a rounded corner. **Artefact** — this is what Step 2 of the original plan targets. |
| 1.06, 0.79, 0.79, 0.75, 0.75 | 5 | sub-pixel stubs at region junctions. Minor, noted below. |

So the chord problem is real but **small**: two distinct facets in the whole
drawing. The original plan over-weighted it.

## Measurement 2 — the actual defect

The white dot is a perfect circle in the source. It is emitted as:

```
M322.49 167.59 c-37.87 8.99-23.55 66.79 14.27 57.07 c36.48-9.38 22.69-65.84-14.27-57.07z
```

**Two cubics. 180 degrees each.** Fitting a least-squares circle to 400 samples
of that path (`.tmp/scripts/circle_error.py`):

- centre (329.21, 196.22), radius 29.37 px
- **max radial deviation 0.580 px = 1.97 % of the radius**

And the theoretical error of approximating a circular arc of angle θ with a
single cubic (`k = 4/3·tan(θ/4)`):

| arc per cubic | error, % of radius | in px at r = 29.4 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **180°** | **1.8350 %** | **0.539** |
| 120° | 0.1542 % | 0.045 |
| 90° | 0.0273 % | 0.008 |
| 60° | 0.0024 % | 0.001 |
| 45° | 0.0004 % | 0.000 |

The measured 1.97 % matches the theoretical 1.84 % for a 180° cubic. **The error
is essentially all angular span, not least-squares quality.** The fit is as good
as a 180° cubic can be; a 180° cubic just cannot be a semicircle.

### Why the current code allows it

The accept test in `fit()` is `err <= tolerance²` with `tolerance = 1.5 px`, an
**absolute** bound. But the error of a wide-angle cubic is **proportional to the
radius**: a 180° cubic on a circle of radius `r` is off by `0.018·r`. It
therefore passes a 1.5 px tolerance for every `r < 83 px`. Every fillet and dot
in a logo is under that, so they are all allowed a single 180° cubic with up to
1.5 px of bulge.

And 1.5 px of *bulge* is not the same as 1.5 px of *noise*. It is a smooth,
low-frequency departure from round, which is precisely what reads as "good, but
not quite perfect" when the source is a perfect circle. This is the user's
complaint, exactly.

## Revised plan

Step order changes; Step A is new and is the fix.

### Step A (new, primary) — cap the turning angle of a single cubic

In `fit()`, before accepting a fitted cubic, measure how much the piece turns
(accumulated angle between successive smoothed tangents along `pts[a..=b]`). If
it exceeds `MAX_TURN`, split at the point of half-turn and recurse, **regardless
of whether the deviation already passes tolerance**.

Proposed `MAX_TURN = 90°`. Justified by the table above: 90° per cubic puts the
shape error at 0.027 % of the radius, which is under a thousandth of a pixel for
anything in a normal drawing — below any plausible tolerance, so the tolerance
test goes back to measuring what it is meant to measure. 120° would also be
defensible (0.15 %) and costs fewer segments; the implementation should measure
both on the corpus and pick with the numbers in hand.

Cost: a full circle goes from 2 cubics to 4. That is the classic four-arc circle
and it is the right answer.

Expected effect on the dot: radial error **0.580 px → ~0.008 px**, a 70x
improvement, for two extra cubics in the whole file.

### Step B — turn-aware straightness test

Unchanged from Step 2 of the original plan, but now correctly scoped: it buys
the two mid-corner facets (11.7 px and 9.6 px), not the bulk of the problem.

### Step C — least-squares tangents

Unchanged from Step 1 of the original plan. Lower priority than first thought,
since Measurement 2 shows the least-squares fit is not the bottleneck. Still
worth doing: better tangents mean fewer error-driven splits, which partly pays
for the splits Step A adds.

### Step D — handle-length guard

Unchanged from Step 4 of the original plan.

### Step E — `SPLINE_TOLERANCE`

**Do not change it.** Original Step 5 proposed re-measuring it; Measurement 2
shows the tolerance was never the problem, and lowering it would only force more
splits for the wrong reason. The constant stays at 1.5 and its doc comment gains
a note that the angular cap, not the tolerance, is what guarantees arc fidelity.

### Step F — sub-pixel stubs

Five oblique stubs under 1.06 px, at region junctions. Out of scope for this
session: they sit exactly where the shared-boundary guarantee is enforced, and
collapsing them needs its own reasoning about both faces. Recorded here so it is
not lost.

### Step G — tests

Replace the segment-count assertion in `un_circulo_sale_liso`, which is the test
that let this through: `circulo.len() <= 12` passes happily on a 2-cubic circle.
Count is the wrong measure. Add instead, on the radius-30 circle:

- fit a least-squares circle to a dense sampling of the emitted path and assert
  **max radial deviation < 0.05 px**. That is the assertion that actually says
  "round", and it fails loudly on today's output (0.58 px).
- assert no oblique line segments in a shape that has no oblique edges.
- keep the existing tangent-continuity check at every joint.

Corpus and golden output will change; regenerate and inspect.

## Revised risk note

File growth is now bounded and predictable: at most one extra cubic per 90° of
arc. On this logo that is a handful of segments on ~2.1 KB. The earlier worry
about curves replacing lines wholesale does not apply, since Measurement 1 shows
only two facets are actually convertible.
